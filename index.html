Driving With an Expired MOT: Rules, Penalties and Exceptions

What happens if a car's MOT runs out, the two narrow exceptions that allow it to be driven, how cameras pick it up and how insurance and tax are affected.

Road Legal · Updated 25 November 2026 · 3 min read

There is no grace period once an MOT certificate expires. From the day after the expiry date, the car should not be driven on public roads except in two specific situations. Plenty of people find out the hard way, often because they simply lost track of the date. Setting an mot due date reminder is far cheaper than any of the consequences below.

How it gets noticed

Police automatic number plate recognition cameras check registrations against MOT, tax and insurance records in real time. A car with no valid MOT can be flagged as it passes a camera, and officers can stop it. Insurers and DVLA can also see MOT status.

The penalties

Driving without a valid MOT can lead to a fine of up to £1,000. If the car also has a dangerous defect, the driver can face a much larger fine, penalty points and a possible driving ban. These are separate from any action over tax or insurance.

The two exceptions

  • Driving to a pre-booked MOT test.
  • Driving to or from a garage to have repairs done that are needed to pass the MOT.

Keep evidence of the booking, such as a confirmation email, in case you are stopped. The exception does not cover a detour on the way.

Insurance

Many insurance policies require the car to have a valid MOT. If it does not, an insurer may refuse or reduce a claim, particularly if the condition of the car contributed to an accident. Even where the policy wording is less strict, a lapsed MOT can complicate a claim.

Tax

In Great Britain, a car that needs an MOT cannot be taxed without one. If the MOT runs out before your tax renewal, you will not be able to renew the tax until the car passes. If the car is going to sit unused, declaring it SORN keeps you within the rules.

Older vehicles

Vehicles more than 40 years old can be exempt from the MOT if they have not been substantially changed. Owners still have a legal duty to keep them roadworthy, and many choose to have a voluntary test each year.

Simple ways to avoid it

  • Set two reminders, one a month before and one two weeks before.
  • Book the test early in the window.
  • Check the date whenever you buy a car.

If you have just realised it has expired

Stop driving the car, apart from to a pre-booked test or to a garage for repairs. Book the test as soon as you can and keep the confirmation. If the car is not needed for a while, you may want to declare it SORN until the test is done. Check your insurance policy wording as well, and tell your insurer if the policy requires it.
